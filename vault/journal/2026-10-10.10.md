# 2026-10-10.10 — KIT-13c: toasts and tooltips

The phase-1 leg of KIT-13: a toast, a notice in a corner over the
surface that goes after a while, and a tooltip, a component's
`accessibility.description` shown in a status line. Story `hotty/toast`,
under Behaviours: the agent shows four toasts as the story opens, one of
each kind; buttons show more (an Undo, two that share an id, one pinned
until Unpin); icon buttons, a field and a switch have descriptions, and a
HottyKeyHints shows them.

**The reference.** OpenTUI has no toast. Its renderer's
`triggerNotification` is the desktop's (OSC 9 and 777), and its
`TerminalConsole` is a log overlay. opencode, built on OpenTUI, draws its
toasts with OpenTUI's boxes: absolute at the top right, on the panel's
colour, between `┃` bars in the kind's colour, padded a row and two
columns, one at a time. `ref/opentui/toast.ts` draws the story's four
that way, stacked so that like sits beside like (`make shot NAME=toast`).
Textual's `notify` gave the rest: kinds by severity, a timeout of five
seconds, a stack.

**A function, not a component** (profile §6.23, instruction 23).
`hottyToast({message, kind, timeout, id, actionLabel, action,
surfaceId})`, `rendererOrAgent`, as `hottyFocus`: a Button's action
calls it, or the agent with `callRendererFunction`. A toast has no place
in the tree, outlives the component that showed it, and as a component
the agent would have to add it and remove it again, and the prompt would
have to say where. `hottyDismissToast({id})` takes one away.
- `kind` is `info` (the default), `success`, `warning` or `error`.
- `timeout` is in milliseconds, 5000 by default; 0 keeps it.
- `id` replaces a toast in place, which gives progress for free
  (`Uploading…` then `Uploaded`), and names it for hottyDismissToast and
  as the action's `sourceComponentId` (`hottyToast:<id>`). Without one,
  `toast-1`, `toast-2`, ….
- `action` is an event only, `{"event": {"name", "context"}}`, and
  `actionLabel` must come with it (`dependentRequired`, both ways). Its
  context is read as the toast shows: Undo names the draft deleted,
  wherever the data model has moved since. A functionCall action would
  need a scope to run in once the Button is gone; left out until wanted.
- `message` and the context take a literal or a path, not a basic
  function: L1, a hotty function's args call hotty functions only
  (`formatString` there fails validation).
- Called by the agent, the toast goes to the surface with the keyboard,
  else the first, its paths resolved at the root, unless `surfaceId`
  names one; by a Button, to its surface, in its scope.

**Time.** A toast is the controller's state (`view.ToastState`), as a
tab shown is, not the data model's. Each rendition counts it down on its
clock as it draws (`TickToasts`): the time between two draws counts only
while nothing held the toast at the first, so a toast under the pointer
or whose action has the keyboard waits. While one counts, the rendition
asks to draw again every
quarter second (`ToastStep`, through `Animating`, as a spinner does), on
the shared clock's steps. Five at most; a sixth takes the oldest's
place.

**Keys and clicks.** A toast's action is a Button: a Tab stop after the
surface's own (and an open Modal's), the newest toast's first; Space and
Enter send its event and dismiss it; its hints are `enter <label>` and
`esc dismiss`. Escape on it dismisses that toast; Escape otherwise,
after a Modal, dismisses the newest, from a text field too, which keeps
the keyboard. A click on the toast dismisses it and gives the keyboard
back, as a click on a box does on a host; a click on its action sends
the event. The keyboard goes with a toast whose action had it.

**Cells.** A box over the top right corner, a column from the edge, as
an open Modal's panel is drawn over the surface: rounded, its border in
the kind's role, its mark the kind's Icon glyph in that role, bold, then
the message, wrapped under itself; the action at the end of the last
row, bold and underlined (reversed in the accent while focused), or on a
row of its own. All as wide as the widest needs, 40 at most. At 80
columns, columns 40 to 78:

```
╭─────────────────────────────────────╮
│ ⓘ Indexing 1,204 files              │
╰─────────────────────────────────────╯
╭─────────────────────────────────────╮
│ ✓ Draft saved                       │
╰─────────────────────────────────────╯
╭─────────────────────────────────────╮
│ ! Disk almost full: 2 GB left       │
╰─────────────────────────────────────╯
╭─────────────────────────────────────╮
│ ✗ Could not reach the server  Retry │
╰─────────────────────────────────────╯
```

Under NO_COLOR the marks say the kind (`ⓘ ✓ ! ✗`, the glyphs of the
`info`, `check`, `warning` and `error` Icons, `view.ToastIcons`). A
field's cursor under a toast is hidden. The column of gutter came from
the reference shot: with the toasts in the frame's last column, the
shot's terminal (wider than the 80 columns `stty` gave Bubble Tea) put
the right border of the rows with a long run of blanks in its own last
column. A box off the edge is how opencode places its toast anyway, and
a terminal's last column is where its writes wrap.

**Text.** After the surface (and an open Modal), after `───`, a line a
toast: `✓ Draft saved`, `✗ Could not reach the server  [ Retry ]`.

**Host, a baseline.** A third top element, `~t`, `div role=region
aria-label=Notifications`, after the layer so that it sits over an open
Modal, fixed at the top right, `min(22rem, 100% − 1rem)` wide. A toast is
`div data-on=click`, `role=alert` for a warning or an error and
`role=status` otherwise; its mark the basic icon in the kind's colour,
`aria-hidden`; its action a borderless button. Filled with a tenth of
the kind's colour, ringed with it. It is cut at the surface's edges;
the toast as its own surface at a higher z is KIT-13h. Checked by
`hotty render` at 1.6x and 56 columns.

**The tooltip** needs no component: `accessibility.description` is
A2UI's own words for what a label doesn't say, and an agent that writes
them for screen readers gets tooltips for nothing. It shows in the
HottyKeyHints, a row over the keys, as a status line: the description
of the element under the pointer, else of the element with the
keyboard, its own or the nearest one around it, in the text's colour and
italic. A key or a click hides the pointer's until the pointer goes to
another element, as a GUI's tooltip goes. Without a description on the
surface there is no row; when there is one, the row stays, blank, so
nothing moves; its width doesn't count toward the HottyKeyHints'.
Focused on the mute button, the HottyKeyHints' two rows are:

```
Silences notifications about this conversation
enter press • ? more
```

The key hints rather than a line of its own: the agent places one
HottyKeyHints at the bottom already, and a surface has no other line
the renderer owns. A floating box at the pointer would cover what is
being read, needs room on every side in a grid, and cannot show for the
keyboard.

**Hover in cells: yes.** The storybook already asks for every move (mode
1003) to set the pointer's shape, so motion events cost nothing more,
and a redraw comes only when the pointer goes onto or off a described
element or a toast (`Rendition.Hover` reports it; `Book.Hover` and
`storybook -bare` redraw then). It also holds a toast while the user
reads it, which the keyboard alone can't do for a toast without an
action. Without mode 1003 the keyboard's description still shows.

**Hover on a host: yes too, by SPEC §9.4.** HOTTY has `hover` now: a
surface placed with `v=1` hears the element under the pointer, and out.
The html rendition takes it (`hovered`): the tip row follows it, and a
toast under it waits. A host's own focus moves (`focus` with `t`) hide
it as a key does in cells. The Book places its surfaces with `v=1` where
the host lists `hover` (hotty-blitz does), so the storybook in hottyterm
has hover tooltips. Each described element also keeps `aria-description`.
No `title` attribute: no HOTTY host draws one, and a browser host would
draw a second tooltip.

**Vectors** (`vectors/keys.yaml`, both renditions, five): Escape
dismisses the newest, a field keeping the keyboard; a click on a toast;
Tab to the action last and Enter (`retry`); Escape on the action
dismissing that toast; a click on Undo with the context read as it
showed (`{draft: d-17}`); an id's toast replaced in place and
`hottyDismissToast`. The player has a new expectation, `toasts`, their
ids newest first.

**Checked:** `make check`; tests for the rows of each kind, roles and
NO_COLOR, the clock and holding, the tooltip row and hover and hush,
the cursor under a toast (cells); the region, roles, the clock, hover
holding, and the tip row by hover and focus (host); the reference shot
beside opencode-style toasts.

**What KIT-13h has left:**
- Toasts as a surface of their own at a higher z (SPEC §5.2), not cut at
  the surface's edges, placed at the program's screen corner rather than
  the surface's, and over the other surfaces.
- A tooltip as a surface beside the element: hover gives a cell, not the
  element's area, so placing it needs the element's box (as a click's
  `area` gives).
- Toasts coming and going by motion on the shared clock (host-motion: no
  CSS animations on a host).
- In a scrolled storybook pane, the cells toasts scroll with the frame,
  as an open Modal's panel does.

**Open, for the maintainer** (proposals):
- The corner is the top right, opencode's; Textual's is the bottom right,
  where in cells it would cover the HottyKeyHints. Proposed to keep.
- ⓘ draws wider than a cell in the shot's font and touches the message.
  It is the `info` Icon's glyph, so the two stay one; if it reads
  cramped, change `icons.Glyph("info")` itself rather than the toast's.
- Five toasts at most, 40 columns (22rem) wide, five seconds by default.
  Proposed to keep.
- Escape in a text field dismisses the newest toast, since nothing else
  takes it there; a field that wants Escape for itself binds it.
  Proposed to keep.
- The host's key hints leave out a toast action's keys, as they leave a
  Button's (the host works buttons itself). Since SPEC §10.1 names the
  focused element, the host could show them for every Button; proposed
  as a follow-up across the kit, not for toasts alone.
- Text writes no descriptions: a pipe has no pointer and no focus.
  Proposed to keep.
- A surface nobody draws for a while (a story the storybook moved away
  from) loses its toasts at its next draw: the gap counts. Counting only
  what was drawn would cap each gap at a step or two, at the price of a
  toast that outlives its time in a program that ignores `Animating`.
  Proposed to keep as is.
