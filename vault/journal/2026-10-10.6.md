# 2026-10-10.6 — KIT-10c: HottyChart and HottySparkline

A phase-1 leg: a sparkline, a braille line chart and bars, in cells, with
a host baseline and text. Stories `hotty/chart` (CPU and memory over 20
minutes with a slider on the newest point, deploys a day as grouped bars,
a balance that goes below 0, a chart waiting for its first point) and
`hotty/sparkline` (CPU by host in a Row of name, sparkline and latest
value; one three rows tall; temperatures below 0; pings with gaps; an
empty one that keeps its width).

**Two components, not one or three.** `HottyChart` with `kind` line or
bar, and `HottySparkline`:
- Line and bar charts share everything an agent writes: series, labels,
  the axis, `window`, `height`. One component with a `kind` keeps the
  data when the agent changes its mind, as a ChoicePicker's `variant`
  does.
- A sparkline shares only its values. It has no axis, labels or legend,
  sits inline in a Row beside text, is a row tall by default, and its
  bottom is 0 or its lowest value rather than a fitted axis. As a `kind`
  of HottyChart, half of the chart's props would mean nothing for it,
  and the instruction would need a paragraph of exceptions.
- Names are the plain nouns after the prefix (catalog-naming). Instructions
  18 and 19, profile §6.18 and §6.19 (the storybook leg took §6.16 and
  the switch 17).

**Props.**
- `values`: one series, numbers and nulls (a missing value), literal or
  bound. Or `series`: each a `label` and its `values`, which may be bound
  one by one (`{"label": "cpu", "values": {"@path": "/cpu"}}`).
  `labels`: a label a point.
- `window`: the last N points, the newest at the right edge. With the
  data model's lack of append (L4), the agent writes the next index and
  the chart slides. L5 is in the instruction: a long series belongs out
  of `sendDataModel`.
- `min`, `max`, `height` (8 for a chart, 1 for a sparkline), `weight`.
- The schema: `values` is an inline `oneOf` of an array of number or
  null, DataBinding and FunctionCall, so the core classifies it Dynamic;
  `series` items are `#/$defs/chartSeries`, a local ref, which stays
  static while the core resolves the paths inside the literal list.

**The axis is the view's** (`view.ChartAxis`), so cells and a host put a
value at the same height and label the same ticks. hotty-go's
`series.Scale` fits it: from 0 when the values are all one sign, round
ends, about `max(3, min(6, height/2))` steps. Cells needs each tick in a
row of its own with a free row between, or the labels read as one. When
the fitted ticks are closer, the view takes the Scale with fewer steps
that spreads them, if it spans at most half as much again; else the
tightest axis, and cells labels 0, the top and the bottom first, then
what has room, as plothot does (web's chart). Labels carry the step's
decimals, and k, M or G once the step reaches one.

**Glyphs in cells.**
- Line: braille, two by four dots a cell (hotty-go's `braille.Canvas`,
  an ink a series). A value's dot row runs from the middle of the bottom
  row to the middle of the top one, so a tick's label is level with its
  value. A null breaks the line; a lone value is a dot.
- Bars: a group a point, a bar a series side by side. 0 can fall inside
  a row (the balance's axis is -250 to 750), and a bar below 0 hangs
  from it. hotty-go's `blocks` only rises from a row's bottom edge, so
  the chart has its own cell rule: eighths (`▁`…`█`) where the bar starts
  at the cell's bottom, `▀` or `▔` where it covers the top (the only
  blocks that hang), at least a mark in the zero row.
- Axis: `┤` on a labelled row, `│` elsewhere, `└───`, in `border`; labels
  `muted`. Legend: `━` (line) or `■` (bars) in the series' colour.
- Sparkline: hotty-go's `blocks.Column`, eighths up `height` rows. Its
  lowest value is `▁`, not blank, so only a missing value is a gap.
- Series colours: info, warning, success, error, round again. Not accent,
  which marks only focus.

**hotty-go.** Used: `braille` (lines), `blocks.Column` (sparkline),
`series.Scale`/`Axis` (fit, ticks, decimals), `chart.Line.Path` and
`chart.DefaultStroke` (the host's SVG path). Not used: `chart.Spark`
(one row, scaled min to max, so it can't start at 0 or be taller),
`blocks.Bar` for chart bars (above), `series.Parser`/`Set`/`Hist` (the
data comes from the data model, not a text stream). hotty-go lacks
nothing the leg needed.

**The host baseline.**
- A line is an inline `svg` a series, one `path` from `chart.Line`, with
  presentation attributes only and `currentColor` from the series' class.
  It is the simplest correct baseline: braille in HTML depends on the
  host font's braille glyphs and on cells lining up, and boxes cannot
  draw a slope. Blitz draws it (`hotty render`, 1.6x, terminal and
  material light). A new point is one `d` attribute's delta a series.
- Bars and the sparkline are boxes placed by percentages, so they don't
  need SVG.
- The `viewBox` is 480 by 20 a row, a guess at the plot's width that the
  markup can't know. A plot much wider or narrower thickens the stroke
  where the line is steep. Sending the real size is KIT-10h.
- The renders found three things. The plot's 1px axes dropped out at 1.6x,
  so they are 2px, as the table's rule is. The absolutely placed tick
  labels gave their column no width, so it is as wide as the widest, in
  `ch`. A sparkline with `justify-content: flex-end` that overflowed
  showed its oldest values in Blitz, so it runs `row-reverse`, newest
  first.

**Text.** A chart is a table of its values, a row a point and a column a
series; a sparkline is its values on a line, `–` for a gap.

**The reference is ntcharts**, not OpenTUI's FrameBuffer as gap-analysis
had it. Bubble Tea has no charts, and ntcharts (NimbleMarkets) is its
chart library, the stack the other references come from, with all
three things. `ref/charts.go` draws the stories' data: a time series
line chart in braille, a bar chart, sparklines. `make shot NAME=chart
ROWS=52` and `make shot NAME=sparkline`. ntcharts v2.2.0 is the newest
that adds no newer module to ref (v2.3.0 and later raise ultraviolet,
x/sys and others). What the shots show:
- ntcharts stacks a bar's series; the kit groups them side by side,
  which compares them better.
- ntcharts has no bars below 0, no legend, and ticks at fractions of the
  range (0, 28, 56, 75); the kit's are round.
- ntcharts' sparkline starts at 0 only and has no gaps.

**No live story.** Stories have no clock for data, so no point arrives
by itself. The Slider bound to `/cpu/19` moves the newest point instead,
and the axis grows once it passes 75.

**Left open.**
- Horizontal bars, areas, scatter, a second axis, stacked bars.
- A time axis that picks round times. The x labels are evenly spread
  points (10:00, 10:05, 10:10, 10:14, 10:19 at 80 columns).
- KIT-10h: the line's real size on a host, values under the pointer.

**Checked:** `make check`; tests for the view (mapping, window, axis,
ticks, labels), cells (lines, bars with negatives, grouped bars, empty,
sparklines, roles), the host (markup, ids, a new point as two `d`
deltas, `No data` gone after a point) and text; both stories by `hotty
render` at 1.6x in terminal and material light; both shots.
