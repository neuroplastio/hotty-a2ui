# 2026-10-10.8 — KIT-23c: drag and drop in cells

The maintainer picked the design the same day (vault drag-and-drop:
model C, both, with P2, a line; Alt with the arrows; no cut and paste).
This leg builds it in the view and in cells, and the keys on a host. The
host's drags are KIT-23h, blocked on G1 and G2 with hotty. Stories under
Behaviours › Drag and drop (a new `topic` of a story, a node of its own
in nav): `hotty/drag-todo` (a HottyList), `hotty/drag-backlog` (a
HottyTable), `hotty/drag-files` (a HottyTree with an empty folder) and
`hotty/drag-kanban` (three templated Lists of cards, and two people to
drop a card on).

**The API** (profile §6.21, instruction 21). A2UI's actions carry no
payload from the renderer (L2), so the renderer writes, then acts, as a
selection does:
- HottyList, HottyTable and HottyTree take `reorderable`, `dragType`,
  `moved` (bound) and `onMove`. The items are written where they are
  bound, the moved one stays selected, the move goes to `moved`'s path,
  then `onMove` runs. The move is `{"item", "from": {"index", "path"},
  "to": {"index", "path"}}`, a tree's places with `parent` too. `item`
  is what `selected` names (a value, a row key), so that the agent finds
  it the way it finds the selection; `path` tells two lists apart.
- A templated basic List can't take new props, so it takes the
  extension's `reorder` (`type`, `moved`, `onMove`), which the catalog's
  `$defs` now describe, with `drag` (`type`, `value`) and `drop`
  (`accepts`, `value`, `action`) for any component. A drop writes what is
  dropped (a source's value, a list item's data) to `drop.value`'s path,
  then runs the action; the item stays, since what a drop means is the
  agent's. Lists of one type take each other's items (S4), so a kanban's
  move is local, and a person who accepts `card` takes a card as data
  (S5).
- A tree's node takes children when `children` is a list: `[]` is an
  empty folder, which cells now draws `▶ archive 0`, as a closed branch.

**The view** (`view/drag.go`) keeps the drag in the controller's state
(`Drag`: the source and item, the type and value, the target and where):
`Lift`, `Over`, `Drop`, `CancelDrag`, `DropTarget` (the innermost element
on the pointer's path that takes the drag) and `MoveKey`. A move is a
cut and a paste on the bound lists, copy on write, with paths adjusted
when a node leaves a list above the one it goes to. After it the
keyboard follows a List's item: its node key holds the item's path, so a
card at `/board/todo/0` that moved is focused at its new key. Moves are
refused into the item itself or under it, and while a filter applies.

**Cells** (`rendition/cells/drag.go`):
- **The press.** A press on what can be dragged arms a drag. It selects
  a list's item at once; what else the click does waits for the
  release (a second click's action, a branch opening, the Button a card
  is). Without that, a drag would run the card's Button or close the
  folder it lifted. A press in a text field (which a drag selects in) or
  on a slider is theirs. Moves and the release go to the pane that took
  the press, in the Book and in `-bare`, since the keyboard may not be
  there (a press on a card's text focuses nothing).
- **The lift.** Once the pointer leaves the item with the button down,
  the item is lifted and shows faint, a tree's node with the nodes under
  it.
- **The line.** Drawn: an underline in the accent along the row above
  the place, and an overline along the row under it. The underline
  takes SGR 58, so it is the accent under text in its own colour, and on
  the blank row between two items (a HottyList with descriptions, the
  row under the status line) it is a clean rule. The overline can't be
  coloured, SGR 58 being the underline's: xterm.js drew it in the text's
  colour, and some terminals have none. So the underline is the line,
  the row above the place being any row (a heading, a table's rule), and
  the overline is kept for the frame's top, that row's text turned
  accent. A row of `─` would move rows, which P2 rules out; a gutter mark
  says "this row" rather than "between", and the gutter is the
  selection's bar. Into a node, and onto a drop target, the accent
  reversed: one look for "in there", one for "between".
- **Where in a row.** A row is a cell high. The storybook asks the
  terminal for SGR-Pixels (DECRQM 1016) and its cell's size (XTWINOPS
  16), sets the mode when both answer, turns reports back into cells,
  and hands the Book a `DragMsg` with how far down the cell the pointer
  is; the mode is reset on the way out. Halves then say before and
  after, thirds into. Without pixels the design said "the side the item
  comes from"; read literally, an item dragged over the next row lands
  where it was, so it could never move one place down. It takes the
  place of the row it is over instead: after it from above, before it
  from below, into a node that takes children, before it from another
  list. Items of several rows have halves by their rows.
- **Escape**, or a release where no line shows, leaves the item where
  it was.

What a drag looks like, the to-do list at 40 columns with "Buy oat milk"
over "Water the ferns" (marks under each row: `~` faint, `_` the
accent's underline):

```
   To do

  6 items

│ Buy oat milk
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  Call the plumber
  Water the ferns
________________________________________
  Renew the passport
```

A key move is the list redrawn: Alt+ArrowDown on KIT-31, the backlog's
first row, puts it second, still selected, and the hints say
`alt+↑/↓ move` (a tree's add `alt+←/→ move out/in`).

**On a host** the drags wait (KIT-23h); the keys don't. A movable
element's `data-keys` give the program Alt+ArrowUp and Alt+ArrowDown (a
tree's all four), and the HTML rendition hands them to `MoveKey` before
a list's own keys. A browser scrolls with none of them, so they reached
the program anyway; bound, they reach it whatever the host does. A card's
Button takes only Space and Enter, so the List's binding covers its
cards. The extensions are otherwise ignored, and the stories render as
before (`hotty render` at 1.6x: the kanban, the tree with its folder
icons).

**Text** draws nothing of it: it is for reading.

**Grips.** None in cells: the whole row is the handle, as it is for a
mouse in a terminal. On a host, always (KIT-23h): a document can't tell
a finger from a mouse, and a grip on every row that moves keeps the
layout one.

**Checked:** `make check`; view tests (halves, thirds, in place, into
itself, a tree's moves by keys, two Lists, a drop target, a drag
source); cells tests on the four stories (a drag, cancels, halves by
`DragAt`, a press that drags or clicks, a card dropped on a person);
vectors for Alt with the arrows on the list, the table, the tree (out,
into the node above, into the empty folder) and a card, in both
renditions; the frames in vhs; real drags on the private headless
display, by a virtual pointer, in kitty and hottyterm, `-bare` and the
Book: the line goes by halves there, so both have SGR-Pixels and answer
the cell size, and a file dropped on a folder's middle goes in.

**Left for KIT-23h, and later:**
- The host's drag, its line and the grip: G1 (where in a target), G2 (a
  target that isn't draggable).
- A closed folder opening while held over it (S2).
- Scrolling while held at a list's edge: a HottyList's pages, a tree or
  table with a `height`, a scroll view.
- A tree's level by the pointer's column after a branch's last child.
- S7 copy and S8 several.

**Proposals for the maintainer:**
- The drop target reversed whole is heavy on a Card (its border too).
  Where the target has a border, its border in the accent instead would
  read as a ring, the tree's "into" staying reversed.
- Spring-loading: open a closed folder after the pointer has rested on
  its middle for 600 ms, on the frame clock the spinner uses.
- Edge scrolling: while a drag is on a list's first or last row and it
  can move that way, scroll a row a tick.
