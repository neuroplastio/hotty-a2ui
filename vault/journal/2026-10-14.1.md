# 2026-10-14.1 — Cut and paste, in cells; DateTimeInput's bounds

The maintainer, after trying copy in the storybook: "Copy is working
well, nice. Now, we need Cut and Paste. You can also fix date bug".

**Cut and paste** (profile §3.7, "Cutting and pasting"), as a page has
them, in the text control that has the keyboard:
- A cut key (`Control+x`, `Meta+x`) puts what is selected on the
  clipboard, as a copy key does, and deletes it. With nothing selected,
  or in a password field, the key is the program's: on a page, cutting a
  Text's selection does nothing.
- A paste key (`Control+v`, `Meta+v`) asks for the clipboard. A program
  in cells can't read it itself: it sends OSC 52's query
  (`cells.OSC52Query`; Bubble Tea's `tea.ReadClipboard`) and the terminal
  answers, or asks the user first (Ghostty's `clipboard-read = ask`), or
  doesn't. `Rendition.PasteWanted` says, once, that a key asked;
  `Rendition.Paste` types the answer in place of the selection.
- The terminal's own paste (Control+Shift+v in Ghostty on Linux,
  Command+v on a Mac) comes as a bracketed paste, `tea.PasteMsg`, and
  goes to `Paste` too: it needs no permission.
- A one-line field drops a paste's line breaks, as HTML's input does; a
  number field keeps what a number has, as typing does; a textarea's
  CRLF become LF.
- The options are `SetCutKeys` and `SetPasteKeys`, beside `SetCopyKeys`,
  and the storybook's `-cut` and `-paste` flags; `""` for none.
- Checked in a pty: Tab into the login form's Username, `a`, Control+v
  sends the query; the answer `xyz` is typed; Shift+Left twice and
  Control+x put `yz` on the clipboard; a bracketed `PASTED` is typed, and
  the whole field then cuts as `axPASTED`.
- Not there: a middle click pasting the primary selection (Linux's), which
  needs a press that places the caret without acting; and the host's
  cut and paste, which hotty carries with its copy.

**DateTimeInput's min and max** (plexos F146): the schema says each is
oneOf a date, a time and a date-time, told apart by `format`, but the
validator never asserted formats, so every string matched all three and
was refused. The compiler now asserts formats, with its own date, time
and date-time: ISO 8601's extended forms as `DateHint` shows them and
HTML's inputs take them (`09:00`, `2026-10-14T09:00`, an offset or none).
JSON Schema's are RFC 3339's, which want seconds and an offset, so that
`09:00` would be none of them. `openUrl`'s `uri` is asserted too now; no
example or story broke.
